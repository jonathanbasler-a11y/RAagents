# Hybrid Regulatory Due-Diligence Team — Digital Mindset Canvas

Participants: Valérie, Jason, Mohan Bogara, Rebecca, Kimia, and Kofi. Names follow spoken introductions; affiliations and surnames have not been independently verified.

Sources: the workshop conversation and two photographs of the Digital Mindset Canvas. See Workshop Transcript for cleaned participant contributions. 

## Executive summary

Create a hybrid regulatory due-diligence team combining a human regulatory lead, human specialists, and specialist AI agents. Gather and assess evidence faster and more consistently, identify risks and mitigations, and enable a concise, confident recommendation that considers regulatory requirements, business strategy, patient needs, and acquisition implications.

The group described rising demand, complex products and modalities, limited expert capacity, and unpredictable requests with one-to-two-week deadlines. Their concerns included missed risks, inappropriate acquisitions, lost opportunities, later CMC problems, and unclear accountability. The proposed response combines accessible data, agents matched to asset stage, expert validation, continuous feedback, training, leadership sponsorship, and governance. Humans retain decision accountability.

Expected benefits include a larger pool of capable program managers, greater organizational confidence in recommendations, and increased throughput. No numerical targets, baseline, pilot asset, implementation owner, or budget were agreed. These are participant perspectives and intended results, not independently verified findings.

## 1 — Initiative

Working name: **Hybrid Regulatory Due-Diligence AI Team**. Enable the regulatory lead to assess acquisition candidates using human and virtual specialists and recommend whether the company should proceed from a regulatory perspective. Match expertise to asset stage, risks, integration implications, corporate strategy, and patient needs.

Names proposed included Increase Efficiency of Due Diligence, Virtual Regulatory Team, Due Diligence Superpower, and Hybrid Approach. The working name is a synthesis rather than a formally selected title. Kofi mentioned high “RPTS” and unmet need. RPTS = regulatory Probability of Technical Success (Kofi confirmed 2026-10-07).

## 2 — Future Results

Timely collection and assessment of clinical, CMC, regulatory, legal, and strategic evidence; clear red flags and integration implications; a lead who feels supported and empowered to deliver a concise, compliant, strategic recommendation. Rebecca explicitly added business and patient context. Later additions: a broader pool of capable program managers, stronger confidence in their work, and greater deal-assessment throughput.

## 3 — Why Now

Demand is rising across asset stages while novel modalities, products, CMC requirements, and regulatory rules become more complex. Requests arrive unpredictably with short deadlines and pull scarce specialists away from existing projects. Risks missed before acquisition can become expensive afterward.

## 4 — Current Results

Best results described:

1. Appropriate acquisitions based on complete clinical, CMC, and regulatory information.
2. Risks identified with workable mitigations.
3. Sound recommendations when the lead has enough time and expertise.

Worst results described:

1. Missed regulatory risks lead to recommending an unsuitable acquisition.
2. Valuable opportunities are lost, or assessments remain incomplete, because of poor review or insufficient time.
3. Later discoveries create costly problems, including CMC issues or an asset the company cannot use as intended.

Participants often described best/worst scenarios rather than measured current performance.

## 5 — Current Behaviors

Helpful: recognize expertise gaps; seek specialists; prepare proactively; obtain stakeholder input; exchange information promptly; discuss risk tolerance and strategy together; own accepted risks and decisions.

Harmful: poor preparation; manual work that favors familiar risks over nuanced risks; failure to hear regulatory input; one function pressuring another despite unresolved concerns; unclear decision ownership; later blame shifting.

## 6 — Current Mindsets

Helpful: confidence in regulatory judgment, openness to specialist support, collaboration, shared responsibility, and planning with future needs in mind.

Harmful: believing one person must do everything; low confidence; acting out of fear; accepting business pressure; siloed thinking; allowing a lifecycle leader to make the regulatory assessment. Some answers described behavior; these are synthesized underlying attitudes, not verbatim quotations.

## 7 — New Behaviors and Capabilities

The lead actively uses virtual and human specialists. The organization makes necessary information accessible to the team for timely collection and evaluation. Expertise reflects early- versus late-stage assets and includes medical-device and labeling expertise where appropriate.

## 8 — New Mindsets

Trust agents while validating outputs with existing regulatory experts. Feed corrections back for continuous improvement. Experts help define agents themselves. Invest quality information and time in agent development. Final decisions remain human. Kofi's desired experience for the lead: “I am fully supported without want.” The feedback mechanism was not specified; it does not imply automatic model retraining.

## 9 — How

**Individual:** Empower the lead to shape agents and make decisions with virtual and human expert support.

**Team:** Upskill people in hybrid ways of working and involve experts in defining agents. Use expert validation and feedback.

**Organization:** Accept and take accountability for the hybrid approach. Preserve human responsibility; “because the machine said so” is insufficient. Leadership creates governance and an enabling ecosystem for fast iteration. Adoption must extend beyond a grassroots effort.
