# Pressure test — Hybrid Regulatory Due-Diligence Team

Internal challenge pass on the Workshop Summary and Workshop Transcript. This distinguishes what the six participants actually contributed from proposals still to be designed. It is not a finding about current company performance.

## Bottom line

The workshop produced a credible direction: a regulatory lead should be supported by human specialists and bounded AI capabilities, with evidence traceability, expert validation, and human accountability. It did **not** yet produce a validated operating model or investment case.

Before this becomes an implementation proposal, fix five weaknesses:

1. **Call it a synthesized canvas, not group consensus.** One Box 4 contribution was lost, several late Box 9 speakers are uncertain, and no formal prioritization or agreement round occurred.
2. **Show the causal chain.** The current synthesis lists good themes but does not prove which mindsets drive which behaviors, or which new behaviors will produce which future results.
3. **Bound the decision.** The hybrid team informs the regulatory dimension of acquisition diligence. It does not make the acquisition decision, and an AI output is not a regulatory recommendation until an accountable human signs it.
4. **Define jobs before agents.** “Specialist AI agents” is still an architecture label. Start with concrete jobs: evidence inventory, source-grounded extraction, gap detection, question routing, precedent retrieval, risk-register drafting, and report assembly. Decide which, if any, warrant separate agents only after workflow design.
5. **Turn aspirations into a pilot hypothesis.** No baseline, target, pilot asset, owner, budget, system boundary, or acceptance threshold was agreed.

## Objections by stakeholder

### Executive sponsor

**Objection 1 — “You have described a compelling future, but where is the business case?”**

**Best response:** “We do not have the business case yet, and I would not pretend that we do. The workshop gave us the value hypothesis: reduce specialist burden, improve consistency, and assess more opportunities without weakening accountability. The pilot has to establish whether those gains are real.”

**Strengthen it by:** Add a one-page pilot scorecard with baseline and target ranges for cycle time, specialist hours, evidence coverage, review rework, material-risk recall, and user confidence. Do not claim savings before measurement.

**Objection 2 — “Are we automating an advisory step or delegating an acquisition decision?”**

**Best response:** “Neither the machine nor the regulatory function owns the acquisition decision. The hybrid team creates a traceable regulatory assessment. The accountable regulatory lead signs that assessment, and the deal governance body owns the final business decision and any accepted risk.”

**Strengthen it by:** Put a decision-rights diagram in the plan: AI drafts and surfaces evidence; specialists validate domain conclusions; the regulatory lead signs the regulatory recommendation; the deal committee accepts or rejects the enterprise risk.

### Finance / business development

**Objection 3 — “Will this slow a one-to-two-week diligence window with new controls and reviewers?”**

**Best response:** “That is a real failure mode. The design only works if controls are embedded in the workflow and review is risk-tiered. We should not require the same depth for every question or asset stage.”

**Strengthen it by:** Define a triage model: minimum evidence pack, red-flag screen, deep-dive triggers, parallel specialist review, and explicit stop/escalate criteria. Measure elapsed time as well as quality.

**Objection 4 — “More throughput sounds attractive, but does it simply encourage us to review more weak deals?”**

**Best response:** “Throughput is not the primary success measure. The aim is better-supported decisions under time pressure. Volume only matters if evidence quality, material-risk detection, and decision confidence hold.”

**Strengthen it by:** Make throughput a secondary measure. Lead with decision quality, evidence completeness, and avoided late surprises. Define a fast exit for clearly unsuitable assets.

### Operations / program leadership

**Objection 5 — “The scarce experts are already the bottleneck. Won’t validation create more work for them?”**

**Best response:** “It could. The pilot must test whether experts spend less time finding and organizing information and more time on judgment. If review effort rises without better decisions, the design has failed.”

**Strengthen it by:** Time-box expert review, measure reviewer hours, use exception-based review where justified, and identify which conclusions always require specialist review. Assign a named process owner and backup.

**Objection 6 — “Who owns the process when evidence is missing, functions disagree, or the deadline arrives?”**

**Best response:** “The regulatory lead owns the assessment workflow, not every underlying fact. Domain specialists own their conclusions; the deal governance body owns accepted uncertainty and the go/no-go decision. Missing evidence and dissent should be visible, not averaged away.”

**Strengthen it by:** Add a RACI, an unresolved-issues register, dissent handling, risk-acceptance authority, and rules for recommendations made under incomplete evidence.

### R&D / technology / data

**Objection 7 — “What exactly is an agent here, and why do we need several?”**

**Best response:** “We have not earned that architecture decision yet. We first define the regulated jobs, inputs, outputs, controls, and handoffs. Some may be simple retrieval or workflow automation; others may need model-assisted reasoning. ‘Agent’ is not the unit of value.”

**Strengthen it by:** Replace a generic agent roster with a workflow decomposition and a build/buy/manual decision for each job. Specify source systems, version control, citations, confidence handling, and fallback behavior.

**Objection 8 — “Can deal-room data, confidential product information, and privileged material safely enter the proposed tools?”**

**Best response:** “Not until the data boundary is approved. The pilot should use an authorized environment and a deliberately limited document set. No public consumer tool, silent model training, or uncontrolled copying.”

**Strengthen it by:** Add data classification, access control, approved hosting/model boundaries, retention and deletion, audit logs, vendor terms, privilege handling, and incident response. Name Information Security, Legal, Privacy, and Records stakeholders.

**Objection 9 — “How will you prevent confident but unsupported conclusions or outdated regulatory precedent?”**

**Best response:** “Every material statement must point to an approved source, and unsupported inference must be labeled. The model may help assemble and challenge an assessment; it cannot manufacture the evidence base.”

**Strengthen it by:** Require claim-level citations, source dates and versions, a separation of fact/inference/recommendation, approved-source retrieval, contradiction checks, and test cases with known traps.

### Quality / regulatory

**Objection 10 — “How can we validate a system whose output depends on changing documents, prompts, and models?”**

**Best response:** “We validate the intended use and controlled workflow, not a promise that a model is always right. The assurance level should match the role: decision support with mandatory human review is different from autonomous decision-making, which is out of scope.”

**Strengthen it by:** Define intended use, prohibited use, versioned prompts/configuration, test sets, acceptance criteria, change control, periodic performance review, deviation handling, and human override. Avoid the undefined phrase “continuous learning”; feedback may improve prompts, knowledge, workflow, or training without automatically retraining a model.

**Objection 11 — “What if experts disagree with each other or with the system?”**

**Best response:** “Disagreement is information. The system should preserve competing interpretations and the evidence behind them. The accountable lead resolves or escalates the issue; the tool does not collapse dissent into artificial certainty.”

**Strengthen it by:** Add documented dissent, escalation thresholds, adjudication ownership, minority views, and a record of why the final position was chosen.

## Logic gaps in the nine-box canvas

- **Box 2 mixes outcomes and mechanisms.** “Collect information” is a behavior/capability; “supported and empowered” is an experience; “sound, timely recommendations” is the outcome. Separate them.
- **Box 4 is scenario-based, not a current-state baseline.** The workshop described plausible best/worst cases but supplied no frequency, severity, cycle-time, rework, or miss-rate evidence.
- **Boxes 5 and 6 overlap.** “Low confidence” appears as both behavior and mindset; “late information” is more a process condition than a mindset. Tighten the categories before presenting the canvas as causal.
- **Boxes 7 and 8 contain solution assumptions.** Willingness to use agents and trust-with-validation are reasonable, but the canvas should also allow a non-agent solution where process, data access, or a checklist solves the job better.
- **Box 9 lacks operating mechanics.** Empowerment, upskilling, acceptance, and governance are themes. They are not yet ownership, controls, cadence, funding, or rollout.

## Recommended framing for Claude’s slide and plan

Use the slide as a faithful workshop artifact, with a subtitle such as **“Synthesized from six participant contributions; to be validated and prioritized.”** Do not overcrowd it with implementation detail.

The implementation plan should open with four explicit statements:

1. **Purpose:** produce a faster, source-grounded regulatory assessment for acquisition diligence.
2. **Boundary:** AI supports evidence work and structured challenge; humans own conclusions, recommendations, and accepted risk.
3. **Pilot hypothesis:** a bounded workflow can reduce elapsed time and specialist search/assembly effort without reducing material-risk detection or evidence traceability.
4. **Gate:** scale only if the pilot meets predefined quality, control, adoption, and efficiency thresholds.

## Evidence status

Supported by the workshop: increasing demand and complexity as participant perceptions; short and unpredictable timelines; scarce specialist capacity; asset-stage-specific expertise; need for accessible information; expert involvement and validation; human accountability; leadership governance; desired confidence and throughput.

Not established: the prevalence or cost of missed risks; current cycle-time baseline; expected time/cost savings; superiority of a multi-agent architecture; validated model performance; approved data environment; named owner; budget; pilot; rollout timing; or numeric success targets.
